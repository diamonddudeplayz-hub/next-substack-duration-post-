## 1. De-polishing — how to make a draft stop sounding like AI

The single strongest "AI tell" in my first drafts is **balanced opposition**: a sentence built as
`not X, but Y`, or two clauses of equal length pivoting on a comma, over and over. One or two land.
A page of them reads like a machine that has learned the shape of insight without the content. The
fix is not to delete them. It is to **break the symmetry** on most of them so the few that remain
actually hit.

Three moves, in order of how often I reach for them:

1. **Collapse the two halves into one plain clause.** If the contrast is not doing real work, cut it.
2. **Unbalance the lengths.** Keep the contrast but make one side much shorter, so it reads like a
   person landing a point rather than a template filling a slot.
3. **Replace the pivot with a full stop.** Two sentences instead of one see-saw.

### Worked examples (pulled from this very draft)

**Example A — the one you liked (collapse + unbalance).**

> Before: *"It is in a view the consensus does not hold yet, plus a reason it cannot hold it."*
>
> After: *"It is in a view the consensus does not hold yet, and a reason it can't."*

The "plus a reason it cannot hold it" was a second balanced limb restating the first. Cutting it to
"and a reason it can't" keeps the idea, drops the symmetry, and the contraction makes it sound said
rather than written. This is the move to apply ~8–10 times across the draft, not everywhere.

**Example B — replace the pivot with a stop.**

> Before: *"That is right, and it stops one step short. The step it misses has a name, and the name
> is duration."*
>
> After: *"That's right, as far as it goes. The step it misses has a name. Duration."*

Same content, but the one-word sentence does the work the balanced clause was faking.

**Example C — leave it alone.**

> *"Being early has cost me a few percent, and nothing of the thesis."*

This one stays. It earns its symmetry because the two halves carry genuinely different weight (money
lost vs. thesis intact), and it is the only one in its paragraph. The rule is not "kill every
parallel construction" — it is "kill the ones that are load-bearing scaffolding rather than the
point itself."

### A checklist for the de-polish pass

- Search the draft for `not X, but`, `isn't X, it's`, `not A, B` and `,  and ` see-saws.
- For each: is the contrast the point, or decoration? Decoration → collapse or unbalance.
- Cap the survivors at roughly one per section.
- Read it aloud. Anything you would not actually say to a person over a drink, loosen.
- Keep every number, source and hedge exactly as is — de-polish is a prose pass, never a facts pass.

## 2. Spines for the posts this one spun off

This post deliberately parked three threads as "its own post." Seeds below so they aren't lost.

### Post A — "What actually makes a catalyst" (the hardest question)

The current post defines a real catalyst (it reclassifies the business *and* removes the specific
reason the consensus hadn't reclassified it already) but explicitly punts on the sequel:

> *"How you tell a real catalyst from a priced-in one before the move, and buy ahead of it, is the
> harder question, and its own post."*

**Spine:** the test is whether the event removes a *named, specific barrier* to the consensus
re-rating — not whether it's "good news." Worked through Itron (industrials analysts with no EV/ARR
model → Investor Day hands them the framework), contrasted with a watched mega-cap where the next
print is already priced. The edge is a view the consensus can't yet hold, plus the dated event that
forces it to. Sizing = (gap to fair value) / (duration-to-catalyst).

### Post B — Wheaton (WPM) as a streamer (the sixth-name aside)

Parked out of this post because it has cash flows and a multiple, so it didn't fit the "off the
axis" gold endpoint:

> *"I hold it mostly through Wheaton, which does have cash flows and a multiple... a separate post."*

**Spine:** the streaming model (upfront capital for a fixed % of future production at a fixed low
cost) as a way to own the gold floor *with* a cash yield and a multiple — i.e. a long-duration bet
on the metal that still sits *on* the duration axis, unlike bullion. Why I pair it with a smaller GLD
position (hedging the company-specific streamer risk on top of the metal price). This is where WPM
becomes its own spectrum point rather than an aside.

### Post C — Does buying quality at the 200-week line actually work?

The current post fires the 200-week rule on exactly one name (Itron) and is careful to call that an
observation, not a test:

> *"One firing across five years is an observation, not a test. A real test... wants fifteen years
> and a cross-section of names, and that is a separate post."*

**Spine:** a real backtest. Fifteen years, a cross-section of quality compounders, 200-week-MA-touch
entries vs. dollar-cost-averaging vs. buy-and-hold. Does the rule add return, and if so, is the edge
concentrated in the higher-duration / more mean-reverting names (as this post's framework predicts)?
Needs price history pulled and verified — a data post, flag the build before starting.
