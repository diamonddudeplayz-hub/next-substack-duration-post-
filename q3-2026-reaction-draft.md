# Itron Guided Earnings Down. The Operating Business Grew 14%.

*The headline fall is a tax comparison plus interest income the company traded for recurring revenue. ARR reaccelerated to 25%, and for the first time the growth showed up in the guide.*

PRIMARY SOURCE ARBITRAGE · October 27, 2026

---

## The short version

- **What happened.** Itron reported Q3 on October 27. Revenue of $599 million grew 3%, its first year-over-year increase since the first quarter of 2025. Annual recurring revenue, the software line, reached $423 million, up 25% and reaccelerating from 21% last quarter. Non-GAAP EPS was $1.94 against a $1.56 consensus. Management raised the full-year EPS outlook for the second straight quarter, to $6.72-$6.82.
- **Why it looks like a decline anyway.** That full-year midpoint, $6.77, is 5% below 2025's $7.13. Normalized for a 2025 tax benefit and falling interest income, it is 14% higher. Interest income fell because the cash that earned it went to two software acquisitions, Urbint and Locusview, which added the recurring revenue.
- **What happens next.** Q4 is guided to $1.70-$1.80 on revenue up 10%. The guide implies gross margin falls from Q3; in each of the last four years it rose. 2027 guidance comes in February. The segment target framework that lets analysts value the software, what I have been calling the forcing function, comes after the Resiliency integration finishes in early January, which on the 2024 precedent points to roughly March.
- **What I hold.** Itron. I added this morning.
- **What would prove me wrong.** ARR growth below 15% for two consecutive quarters. It is 25%.

## The reported quarter

Revenue was $599 million, up 3%. ARR was $423 million, up 25%. The quarter that mattered for the thesis was the one before this one: in Q2, ARR growth fell to 21% from 28% in Q1, and the question was whether that was a single soft print or the start of a trend. The build since management began disclosing the metric: $368 million at the end of 2025, $414 million in Q1, $417 million in Q2, $423 million now. Q3 answered the question in the direction I needed, and the year-over-year rate went up, not down.

Adjusted EBITDA was $115 million, a 19.1% margin, a quarterly record. Non-GAAP earnings were $1.94 per share against $1.54 a year ago. The number to hold onto is not the year-over-year figure but the one against expectations: the consensus was $1.56, the mean of eight estimates on LSEG. That is a 24% beat. It is also the third of its size this year. Q1 was $1.49 against $1.25. Q2 was $1.59 against $1.29. Q3 was $1.94 against $1.56. The analysts covering this company have under-forecast it every quarter of 2026, and the misses have not gotten smaller as the year went on.

That record is worth more to the thesis than any single multiple, because it is the gap I write about caught in the act. The people whose models set the price keep building the hardware company and keep getting surprised by the earnings.

## The headline says earnings fell

Here is the number I went to the slides for. On its full-year outlook, Itron guided non-GAAP EPS to $6.72-$6.82. The company's own slide 17 describes the midpoint as "down $0.36 or 5% vs 2025," and then, on the next line, "Normalized for tax rate and interest income, up $0.81 or 14% at the midpoint." Both sentences are management's. The fourth-quarter slide says the same thing louder: $1.70-$1.80 is "down $0.71 or 29% vs Q4 2025," and normalized, "up $0.14 or 9%."

Two things sit between the headline and the operating business. The first is tax. The 2025 base of $7.13 carried a fourth-quarter tax benefit; 2025 Q4 earnings were $2.46 a share, on a net margin of 17.8%, higher than any operating quarter around it. The second is interest income, and it is the more interesting of the two. It fell from $13.6 million in Q3 2025 to $7.2 million this quarter, a $6.4 million drop, because the company spent its cash balance on Urbint and Locusview. Those are the acquisitions that built the Resiliency segment, the one printing 74% gross margins. So the line depressing the reported earnings is the same decision that added the recurring revenue. Hooper walked the bridge on the call (Joan Hooper, Q3 2026 earnings call, October 27, 2026): operating income added $0.38 a share, interest income subtracted $0.14, and taxes and share count added $0.16. The operating line did the work, against an interest-income drag the company chose.

Now set it against what the Street was carrying. The consensus full-year estimate going into the print was $6.37, which is 10.7% below 2025. The sell-side was modeling a double-digit earnings decline. The guided midpoint of $6.77 is not just above that mean; it is above the prior high estimate on the sheet, $6.72. The entire guided range sits at or above where the single most bullish analyst had been. A company does not clear the top of the distribution on a quarter the market reads as a decline unless the market is reading the wrong line.

## Where the margin actually came from

Adjusted gross margin was 41.3%, up 350 basis points. I did not want to take "favorable mix and operational efficiencies," the language in the release, at face value, so I split the move by segment. Device Solutions gross margin rose 450 basis points, Networked Solutions 250, Outcomes 110, and Resiliency entered the comparison at 74.4%. Weighting those, about 262 of the 354 basis points came from segments earning more on their own revenue, and roughly 246 of that came from the two hardware segments. About 91 basis points came from Resiliency's mix. So three-quarters of the gross margin gain this quarter was the hardware business getting cheaper to build and better priced, not the software taking over the mix. Device is the clean read, because it is almost entirely product: its gross margin went from 27.2% to 35.4% over two years, and no mix shift explains that.

That cuts against the lazy version of my own thesis, so I want to be straight about it. This quarter's margin was mostly a hardware story. It is also where the one real new risk sits. Deitrich, same call: "Memory and selected component costs are rising, and we have kept pace with them as third quarter gross margin demonstrates." The memory shortage that has Micron reporting 87% gross margins is now a cost on Itron's bill of materials. Memory is a small part of what goes into a meter, and they offset it this quarter with pricing and buffering. But it is the line that can stall the hardware half of the margin gain, and I will be watching one number for it next quarter, which I come to below.

## Bookings, and the quarter the guide is hiding

Bookings were $482 million, a third-quarter record, up 27% year over year. Book-to-bill was 0.80, which reads soft until you notice it is a record bookings quarter divided by a higher revenue number. On a trailing-twelve-month basis book-to-bill is 0.97. Fourth quarter is the seasonal peak for orders: bookings were $1,403 million in Q4 2024 and $737 million in Q4 2025. Deitrich: "October is off to a really nice start." I do not put weight on that sentence by itself, but the seasonality is in the data, not the sentiment.

The part of the guide worth reading closely is the margin it implies. Fourth-quarter EPS of $1.70-$1.80 on revenue up about 5% sequentially backs out to roughly a 15.5% operating margin, down from 17.8% this quarter. For a margin to fall while revenue rises, either operating expense jumps or gross margin gives back. Operating expense does step up in Q4 every year, for the customer event and sales compensation on the big bookings quarter, and Hooper said as much. But gross margin is the tell, and here the calendar argues against the guide: from Q3 to Q4, adjusted gross margin rose in 2022, 2023, 2024 and 2025, by between 160 and 300 basis points. The only year it fell was 2021, the supply-chain trough. Put the normal Q4 operating-expense step on top of the normal Q4 gross-margin rise and the guide looks like the same conservative setup that preceded the last three beats. I think it is sandbagged, as the prior three were.

The honest version of that call is the symmetry: the one way the guide is not conservative is if gross margin falls in Q4 for the first time since 2021. If it does, that is the component-cost pressure arriving, and the margin read in the section above gets weaker, not stronger. So the Q4 gross-margin line is the single datapoint I am waiting on. It settles both questions at once.

## What a 13-times multiple is paying for

At about $90, on roughly $7 of forward earnings, Itron trades near 13 times. The S&P 500 trades near 20. A company that used to carry a premium to the market now carries a discount of about a third. Leave the software-valuation argument aside for a moment, because this part does not need it.

Deitrich, on the call, called the investment utilities have to make "in our view, inevitable," and said "intelligence is the fastest and least expensive capacity still available." Take that at face value and the question writes itself: why does a company in front of a demand build it calls inevitable trade at a no-growth multiple? The honest answer is that revenue went sideways for two years while management called demand strong. It was $615 million in the third quarter of 2024 and $599 million now. The large conversions have always been described as ahead. On gas, this quarter, Deitrich: "It has not yet shown itself in the bookings and the P&L in any significant way. That's still ahead of us." So the market is not asleep. It has heard "inevitable" for eight quarters and is waiting to see the revenue before it pays for it. Two flat years earned that discount.

What changed this quarter is that the revenue showed up in the guide, not just the pipeline. Fourth-quarter revenue is guided up 10% year over year at the midpoint, the first real acceleration. Bookings were a record. Deitrich named live deployments in the Caribbean, the Southeast and the Northeast behind the sequential growth. None of that requires a software framework or an Investor Day. If Q4 prints up 10% and February's 2027 guide carries a growth number instead of a flat one, the premise under the 13 times, that this is an ex-growth business, breaks on the earnings alone. That is a re-rating that can happen before the one I have been writing about, and on plainer evidence.

The software version still stands, and I will not rebuild it here because I have in prior posts: strip the hardware at one times revenue and the market is valuing the recurring layer below eight times ARR. For contrast, Badger Meter, a water-meter company whose earnings are set to grow about 2%, trades at 32 times earnings against Itron's 13, in the same industry, with its own software and services layer. The market will pay a software multiple for a meter company. It has just decided it needs the framework first, and the framework has a date on it now.
