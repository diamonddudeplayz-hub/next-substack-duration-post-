# Itron Guided Earnings Down. The Operating Business Grew 14%.

*The headline fall is a tax comparison plus interest income the company traded for recurring revenue. ARR reaccelerated to 25%, and for the first time the growth showed up in the guide.*

PRIMARY SOURCE ARBITRAGE · October 27, 2026

---

## The short version

- **What happened.** Itron reported Q3 on October 27. Revenue of $599 million grew 3%, its first year-over-year increase since the first quarter of 2025. Annual recurring revenue, the software line, reached $423 million, up 25% and reaccelerating from 21% last quarter. Non-GAAP EPS was $1.94 against a $1.56 consensus. Management raised the full-year EPS outlook for the second straight quarter, to $6.72-$6.82.
- **Why it looks like a decline anyway.** That full-year midpoint, $6.77, is 5% below 2025's $7.13. Normalized for a 2025 tax benefit and falling interest income, it is 14% higher. Interest income fell because the cash that earned it went to two software acquisitions, Urbint and Locusview, which added the recurring revenue.
- **What happens next.** Q4 is guided to $1.70-$1.80 on revenue up 10%. The guide implies gross margin falls from Q3; in each of the last four years it rose. 2027 guidance comes in February. The segment target framework that lets analysts value the software, what I have been calling the forcing function, comes after the Resiliency integration finishes in early January, which on the 2024 precedent points to roughly March.
- **What I hold.** Itron. I added this morning.
- **What would prove me wrong.** ARR growth below 15% for two consecutive quarters. It is 25%.

---

For readers new to the series, the case in one paragraph. Itron makes the meters and network equipment utilities run their grids on, and it is turning that installed base into recurring software, analytics and services revenue. It began reporting that as annual recurring revenue, ARR, only four quarters ago. The market still prices Itron as a hardware company, near 13 times earnings, while the recurring line grows in the low twenties and carries much higher margins than the hardware. The re-rating I am waiting for comes when management gives analysts the segment-level targets needed to value the software on its own, which I expect at an investor day in early 2027. This post is what the third quarter did to that case.

## The reported quarter

Revenue was $599 million, up 3%. ARR was $423 million, up 25%. The quarter that mattered for the thesis was the one before this one: in Q2, ARR growth fell to 21% from 28% in Q1, and the question was whether that was a single soft print or the start of a trend. The build since management began disclosing the metric: $368 million at the end of 2025, $414 million in Q1, $417 million in Q2, $423 million now. Q3 answered the question in the direction I needed, and the rate went up.

![ARR build](charts/itri-arr-build.png)
*Itron's annual recurring revenue by quarter, with year-over-year growth. The Q2 slip to 21% from 28% did not become a trend. Source: Itron earnings releases, 4Q25 to 3Q26.*

Adjusted EBITDA was $115 million, a 19.1% margin, a quarterly record. Non-GAAP earnings were $1.94 per share against $1.54 a year ago. The number to hold onto is not the year-over-year figure but the one against expectations: the consensus was $1.56, the mean of eight estimates on LSEG. That is a 24% beat. It is also the third of its size this year. Q1 was $1.49 against $1.25. Q2 was $1.59 against $1.29. Q3 was $1.94 against $1.56. The analysts covering this company have under-forecast it every quarter of 2026, and the misses have not gotten smaller as the year went on.

![EPS beats](charts/itri-eps-beats.png)
*Non-GAAP EPS, consensus mean versus reported, for each quarter of 2026. Source: consensus from LSEG; reported from Itron earnings releases.*

That record is worth more to the thesis than any single multiple, because it is the gap I write about caught in the act. The people whose models set the price keep building the hardware company and keep getting surprised by the earnings.

## The headline says earnings fell

Here is the number I went to the slides for. On its full-year outlook, Itron guided non-GAAP EPS to $6.72-$6.82. The company's own slide 17 describes the midpoint as "down $0.36 or 5% vs 2025," and then, on the next line, "Normalized for tax rate and interest income, up $0.81 or 14% at the midpoint." Both sentences are management's. The fourth-quarter slide says the same thing louder: $1.70-$1.80 is "down $0.71 or 29% vs Q4 2025," and normalized, "up $0.14 or 9%."

Two things sit between the headline and the operating business. The first is tax. The 2025 base of $7.13 carried a fourth-quarter tax benefit; 2025 Q4 earnings were $2.46 a share, on a net margin of 17.8%, higher than any operating quarter around it. The second is interest income, and it is the more interesting of the two. It fell from $13.6 million in Q3 2025 to $7.2 million this quarter, a $6.4 million drop, because the company spent its cash balance on Urbint and Locusview. Those are the acquisitions that built the Resiliency segment, the one printing 74% gross margins. So the line depressing the reported earnings is the same decision that added the recurring revenue. Hooper walked the bridge on the call (Joan Hooper, Q3 2026 earnings call, October 27, 2026): operating income added $0.38 a share, interest income subtracted $0.14, and taxes and share count added $0.16. The operating line did the work, against an interest-income drag the company chose.

![EPS bridge](charts/itri-eps-bridge.png)
*Itron's non-GAAP EPS year-over-year bridge: operating income added $0.38, interest income subtracted $0.14, taxes and share count added $0.16. Source: Itron Q3 2026 earnings presentation, slide 10.*

Now set it against what the Street was carrying. The consensus full-year estimate going into the print was $6.37, which is 10.7% below 2025. The sell-side was modeling a double-digit earnings decline. The guided midpoint of $6.77 is not just above that mean; it is above the prior high estimate on the sheet, $6.72. The entire guided range sits at or above where the single most bullish analyst had been. A company does not clear the top of the distribution on a quarter the market reads as a decline unless the market is reading the wrong line.

## Where the margin came from

Adjusted gross margin was 41.3%, up 350 basis points. The release credits "favorable mix and operational efficiencies." I split the move two ways to see which.

By revenue line, both halves got more profitable. Product gross margin rose from 32.6% to 38.1% over the last two years. Service gross margin, the recurring software and analytics line, rose from 43.9% to 53.2%, and from 50.9% a year ago. So the services the thesis rests on are not only growing as a share of revenue, from 12.5% to 18.4%; they earn more on each dollar than they used to. A mix-only story misses that second part.

By segment, the hardware did most of the lifting this quarter, because it is most of the revenue. Device Solutions gross margin rose 450 basis points and Networked Solutions 250, and those two segments are about 80% of the company. Weighting the four segments, roughly 260 of the 350 basis points came from each segment earning more on its own revenue, most of it from the two hardware segments, since they are the bulk of the sales. Resiliency, the 74% gross-margin software segment, added about 90 on mix. The hardware getting cheaper to build and better priced has run alongside the software line getting more profitable for two years, and both are in this quarter.

The one new risk sits in that hardware half. Deitrich, same call: "Memory and selected component costs are rising, and we have kept pace with them as third quarter gross margin demonstrates." The memory shortage that has Micron reporting 87% gross margins is a cost on Itron's bill of materials now. Memory is a small part of what goes into a meter, and they offset it this quarter with pricing and buffering. But it is the line that can stall the hardware side of the margin, and it is the number I am waiting on in Q4, which I come to below.

## Bookings, and the quarter the guide is hiding

Bookings were $482 million, a third-quarter record, up 27%. Book-to-bill was 0.80, which reads soft until you see it is a record bookings number divided by a higher revenue number; on a trailing-twelve-month basis it is 0.97. The fourth quarter is the peak quarter for orders: bookings were $1,403 million in Q4 2024 and $737 million in Q4 2025. Deitrich said October is "off to a really nice start," the kind of line management always offers, so I weight the pattern in the data rather than the sentence.

The part of the guide worth reading closely is the margin it implies. Fourth-quarter EPS of $1.70-$1.80 on revenue up about 5% sequentially backs out to roughly a 15.5% operating margin, down from 17.8% this quarter. For a margin to fall while revenue rises, either operating expense jumps or gross margin gives back. Operating expense does step up in Q4 every year, for the customer event and sales compensation on the big bookings quarter, and Hooper said as much on the call. But gross margin is the tell, and the calendar argues against the guide: from Q3 to Q4, adjusted gross margin rose in 2022, 2023, 2024 and 2025, by 60 to 280 basis points. The only year it fell was 2021, when the component shortage was at its worst. Put the normal Q4 operating-expense step on top of the normal Q4 gross-margin rise and the guide looks like the same conservative setup that preceded the last three beats. I think it is sandbagged, as those were.

The honest version of that call is the symmetry: the one way the guide is not conservative is if gross margin falls in Q4 for the first time since 2021. If it does, that is the component-cost pressure arriving, and the margin read in the section above gets weaker. So the Q4 gross-margin line is the single datapoint I am waiting on. It settles both questions at once.

## What a 13-times multiple is paying for

At about $94, on forward earnings near $7, Itron trades about 13 and a half times. The S&P 500 trades near 20. Over the last decade Itron's forward multiple has averaged a small premium to the market; against the market's 20 it now sits about a third below. Leave the software-valuation argument aside for a moment, because this part does not need it.

Deitrich, on the call, called the investment utilities have to make "in our view, inevitable," and said "intelligence is the fastest and least expensive capacity still available." Take that at face value and the question writes itself: why does a company in front of a demand build it calls inevitable trade at a no-growth multiple? The honest answer is that revenue went sideways for two years while management called demand strong. It was $615 million in the third quarter of 2024 and $599 million now. The large conversions have always been described as ahead. On gas, this quarter, Deitrich: "It has not yet shown itself in the bookings and the P&L in any significant way. That's still ahead of us." So the market is not asleep. It has heard "inevitable" for eight quarters and is waiting to see the revenue before it pays for it. Two flat years earned that discount.

What changed this quarter is that the revenue showed up in the guide, not just the pipeline. Fourth-quarter revenue is guided up 10% year over year at the midpoint. Some of that rate is an easy comparison against last year's trough of $572 million, and the guided dollar figure is really a recovery: at $628 million it is the first quarter back above where revenue sat in the middle of 2025, not a breakout past it. The direction turned, though, and the inventory backs it. Finished goods fell to $37 million, a two-year low, while raw materials rose 14% in the quarter to $229 million, the most components Itron has held against its product sales in two years. A company bracing for weak demand builds finished goods it cannot sell, and these did the opposite. I would not lean on that line alone, because some of the component build is the buffering against the memory shortage Deitrich described, not demand. Bookings were a record, and Deitrich named live deployments in the Caribbean, the Southeast and the Northeast behind the growth. None of that requires a software framework or an investor day. If Q4 prints up 10% and February's 2027 guide carries a growth number instead of a flat one, the premise under the 13 times, that this is an ex-growth business, breaks on the earnings alone. That is a re-rating that can come before the one I have been writing about, and on plainer evidence.

![Inventory](charts/itri-inventory.png)
*Itron inventory components and product revenue. Raw materials at a two-year high against sales, finished goods at a two-year low, as revenue turns up. Source: Itron 10-Q and 10-K filings, 1Q24 to 3Q26.*

The software version of the case still stands, and I will not rebuild the arithmetic here because earlier posts have it. In short: value Itron's hardware at one times its revenue, a cheap price for a meter maker, and what is left of the market value works out to under eight times the recurring software revenue, when software companies that grow the way this line does trade well above ten. For a same-industry contrast, Badger Meter, a water-meter company whose earnings are set to grow about 2%, trades at 32 times earnings against Itron's 13, with its own software and services layer. The market will pay a software multiple for a meter company. It has just decided it needs the framework first, and the framework has a date on it now.

## Close

The stock dipped to $85.25 around midday, then closed at $94.55, up 7.77%. The morning was the headline: a 5% earnings decline and a soft fourth-quarter guide. The afternoon was the market reading the second line on the slide. I added this morning. But $94.55 is still near 13 and a half times forward earnings, back to where the stock sat in September and about a third below where it was a year ago. The day repriced the beat. It did not re-rate the business. That comes on one call in February: Q4 gross margin, for whether the component cycle is biting, and the 2027 guide, for whether the growth is real. I'm holding for both.
