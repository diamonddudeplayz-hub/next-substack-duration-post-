**Body:**

Flag before anything: I'm long ITRI and added yesterday, so read this with that in mind.

Itron reported Q3 yesterday and the headline is strange enough that I want to see if people here read it the way I do or think I'm talking myself into it.

Full-year EPS guide went up for the second quarter in a row, to $6.72-6.82. But the midpoint still screens as down about 5% versus last year, and the Q4 line screens down 29%. So every screener and a lot of the coverage reads it as earnings falling. The stock actually dipped to around $85 intraday before closing up ~8%.

Here's the part I can't get past. The thing dragging the reported number down is mostly two things that aren't the business: a tax benefit in last year's Q4 that doesn't repeat, and interest income falling. And the interest income fell because they spent the cash pile on two software acquisitions (Urbint and Locusview) that added the recurring revenue. So the line making earnings look like they're shrinking is the same decision that bought the growth. Management's own slide normalizes it to +14%. Normalize just for the stuff that isn't operating and the quarter is up, not down.

On the operating side it was a good quarter. Recurring revenue (ARR) hit $423M, up 25%, reaccelerating after a scare last quarter when it dropped to 21%. Non-GAAP EPS $1.94 vs $1.56 consensus, which is the third ~20%+ beat in a row this year. Adjusted gross margin 41.3%.

Now the thing I actually can't resolve, and this is where I want people to push back.

The ARR growth is flattered by the acquisitions. The sequential adds are small, $3M then $6M the last two quarters, and the big jump earlier was the acquired stuff (Resiliency) entering the base. If I run it forward at ~$6M/quarter of adds, reported ARR growth decelerates to like 5-6% through 2027 once the acquisitions sit in both periods. Organic is probably more like 4-5% right now. So the 25% is real but it's about to lap into something that looks a lot worse mechanically, not because the business slowed. My own break condition (ARR under 15% for two quarters) technically trips in 2027 on that lap alone, which to me means the rule is measuring the wrong thing once the comps distort.

So I honestly don't know how to grade the ARR over the next year until they give segment-level targets at an investor day (I think around March). That's the real open question for me: is the recurring engine actually compounding, or am I crediting acquisition math as organic growth?

I'm being careful about the margin beat too. When I decompose it, roughly 75% of the gross-margin gain this quarter was hardware getting more profitable, not the software mix. So the EPS beats prove analysts under-model the whole operation, not specifically that they're missing the software. Those are different claims and I don't want to blur them.

Where I come out: the Q4 revenue guide implies about $628M, which would be the highest quarterly revenue they've done in the five years I track, after two flat years. If that prints and 2027 guidance in February carries a growth number, the "ex-growth industrial at 14x" framing breaks on plain revenue, before any software re-rate.

So the question I'll put to the room: which re-rates this first, the investor day framework that lets you actually value the software, or the revenue just inflecting on its own and forcing it? And separately, if you've tried to model the organic ARR, am I being too generous calling it 4-5%?
