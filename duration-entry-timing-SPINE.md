## The one idea (say this in a sentence, then spend the post proving it)

**"When do I buy" is a duration question wearing a technical-analysis costume.** The value of
timing your entry is proportional to a stock's *duration* times the *volatility of the discount

## Define the term once (your style demands this, briefly, early)

Two things get called "duration" and the post should name both so they don't blur:

- **Equity duration** — how far out the cash flows sit. A high-multiple, low-near-term-FCF stock
  is *long duration*: most of its value is in distant years, so it moves hard when the discount
  rate `r` moves. A high-FCF-yield name with cash back soon is *short duration*.
- **Thesis duration** — how long until the market recognises the value (the catalyst clock).

They're linked: long equity duration usually means the return depends on a re-rating, which means
you're waiting on a clock. The spine uses equity duration as the spine and brings thesis duration
in through catalysts.

## The engine: decompose the return (everything hangs off this)

Total return over a holding period ≈
1. **starting FCF / shareholder yield**
2. **+ growth in per-share cash flow**
3. **+ change in the multiple** (the re-rating or de-rating)

(1) and (2) accrue with *time* and barely care what day you bought. (3) is entirely a function of
the price you pay versus the price you exit.

**So entry timing only ever captures component (3).** The worth of timing = the share of your
expected return that lives in (3), times how mean-reverting or catalyst-driven that re-rating is.

Duration tells you how big (3) is and how violently it swings:
- Long duration → more of the value sits in the multiple, and the multiple is more `r`-sensitive →
  (3) is large and jumpy → **timing matters a lot.**
- Short duration → (1)+(2) dominate, the multiple is a small stable slice → **timing is near-noise.**

And the regime sets the volatility of `r`. Which is where this plugs into work you've already published.

## FOMO, redefined (not a feelings section)

FOMO is a **duration error**, not a psychology failure: paying peak multiple for peak-duration cash
flows right after component (3) already happened for someone else. It hurts most on long-duration
names because that's where the multiple can fall furthest when `r` normalises. The Itron case above
is the clean counter-example (pop was earnings, not multiple → not FOMO yet). Define it this way and
it stops being a cliché and starts being a test you can run.

## Catalysts & conviction sizing

A dated catalyst **shortens thesis duration**: it pulls the re-rating forward in time, which raises
the annualised value of component (3) and cuts how long your capital sits dead.

- Itron Investor Day: a forcing function with an approximate date. Your Burry ledger (puts expiring
  Sept 2027) and Cheniere (Q1 print May 7) are the same move — **a clock on the duration.**
- Conviction sizing falls straight out: **size on (margin of safety) / (duration-to-catalyst).**
  Big gap + short, dated clock → size up. Big gap + no clock (Adobe, gold) → size for patience, not
  for timing.
